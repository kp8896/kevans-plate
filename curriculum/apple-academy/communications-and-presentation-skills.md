## Module 2 — PREPARE (the thinking before the slides)

**Big idea:** a good presentation is *designed backward from a purpose and an
audience*, not forward from your slides.

- **2.2 Story & purpose** — Before anything, answer three questions: *What's my
  message? Why does it matter? What do I want to happen next?* Define the
  **purpose** (why you're speaking) and the **outcome** (the action you want), then
  build the story to serve that outcome. Review and cut anything that doesn't.
- **2.3 Defining your narrative** — Give the talk a clear **structure** (beginning →
  middle → end). Share *your* perspective, state the narrative **clearly and
  briefly**, back it with **facts** (especially for technical topics), and **frame**
  it so the audience knows how to interpret it.
- **2.4 Knowing your audience** — Identify *who* they are and what they care about;
  **adjust the message** to them; avoid jargon that confuses part of the room;
  deliberately **decide what to include and leave out** based on their needs.
- **2.5 Presenting solutions, not just problems** — Never just hand over a problem.
  Always include a **recommendation**, consider **other viewpoints**, and **answer
  the obvious follow-up questions** before they're asked. Practice reframing
  problems as solutions.

**Toby's one-liner:** *Purpose → Audience → Narrative → Recommendation.* If you
can't name all four before you open Keynote, you're not ready to build slides.

## Module 3 — PRESENT (delivery)

**Big idea:** delivery is not decoration — it's half the message. The idea and how
you carry it are weighted equally.

- **3.2 Presentation as an aid** — Slides are a **tool that supports you**, not the
  show. Use data/visuals for impact, tell a **visual story**, and avoid the classic
  mistakes (wall of text, reading slides, too many slides).
- **3.3 Body language** — People read you on two axes: **warmth** (friendly) and
  **competence** (capable). Aim high on both. Show confidence physically (posture,
  eye contact, smile, purposeful hand movement, no fidgeting). Use your **voice** as
  an instrument: loudness, tone/range, articulation, and speed/pauses.
- **3.4 Visuals & graphics** — Slides should be **simple and clear**; graphics
  **professional and well-labeled**; limit raw data and **show the meaning**, not the
  spreadsheet. Always review visuals before presenting.

**Toby's one-liner:** *Be warm AND competent; let the slides serve you; show the
meaning, not the data dump.*

## ✍️ "Your turn" practice checklist (do these for real)
- [ ] Pick a real work topic; write its **purpose + desired outcome** in one sentence each.
- [ ] Draft a 3-part narrative for it (beginning/middle/end) with one supporting fact.
- [ ] Rewrite it for a *specific* audience (e.g., your plant manager vs. an Apple engineer).
- [ ] Turn a current problem at work into a **recommendation** with one counter-viewpoint addressed.
- [ ] Record yourself delivering it once; check warmth, competence, and voice.
